03
Structural Failures in RM-Driven Conversion
// timing
82%
Leads go cold before first call
Weekend campaigns generate 50+ leads overnight. They sit untouched until Monday morning. Industry data shows a 9× drop in conversion beyond 5 minutes — Rupeezy's default is hours or days.
// language
20+
Major Indian languages unserved
RMs speak 1–2 languages. A Hindi-speaking lead who receives a formal English pitch disconnects in 15 seconds. The AP program's addressable market is far larger than the RM team can reach.
// capacity
1:1
One RM, one call, one queue
A campaign generating hundreds of leads overnight fills a multi-day queue. By the time the RM reaches lead #200, the lead has forgotten they even expressed interest.

Handoff Design — Hot, Warm & Cold
// score ≥ 70 · hot leads
RM Handoff
Full conversation context delivered to the RM dashboard before they pick up the phone — transcript, objections raised, objections cleared, interest score, stated network size, and a recommended next step. The RM walks in informed, not blind.
Instant queue
// score 40–69 · warm leads
WhatsApp Fallback
Auto-send personalised WhatsApp message with RISE Portal sign-up link immediately post-call. The message references what was actually discussed — not a generic blast. Warm leads are nudged, not forgotten.
Auto-send post-call
// score < 40 · cold leads
Nurture Queue
Logged with full call summary. Re-queued for agent re-engagement after 14 days. Multi-turn memory ensures the agent remembers prior call context — no repeated openers, no wasted seconds re-pitching what was already heard.
Re-engage +14 days
07
Risks, Trade-offs & Mitigations
// risk-01 · latency
Voice Round-Trip Latency
STT → LLM → TTS pipeline introduces 1–3 seconds of latency per turn. In voice conversations, this reads as unnatural silence and kills trust.
Streaming TTS output while LLM generates. Filler acknowledgement tokens ("Haan, samajh gaya...") bridge the gap. Target <800ms perceived latency.
// risk-02 · code-mixing
Hinglish & Code-Mixing Detection
Indian speakers naturally mix Hindi, English, and regional variants mid-sentence. Binary language detection fails on code-mixed speech — the model either responds in the wrong register or breaks entirely.
IndicLID runs at the sentence level, not call level. The LLM prompt instructs it to mirror the lead's exact code-mixing pattern rather than normalise to a single language.
// risk-03 · hallucination
LLM Hallucinating Product Facts
The agent pitching incorrect brokerage percentages, wrong payout timelines, or fabricated eligibility criteria would create compliance exposure and destroy credibility.
All product claims are RAG-grounded from Appendix A. The system prompt instructs the LLM to refuse to answer product questions not present in the knowledge base.
// risk-04 · consent
TRAI & Consent Compliance
Outbound AI voice calls to unconsented leads may violate TRAI's TCCCPR regulations. Regulatory exposure is material if the agent contacts numbers on the DND registry.
Lead ingestion pipeline scrubs against NDNC registry before agent dispatch. Agent opens with an explicit AI disclosure. All calls are recorded and stored with consent timestamps.

Appendix A — Telecalling Script & FAQ Base Knowledge
// opening
Call opening
Open fast, identify Rupeezy, and ask for a short window of attention before moving into the purpose of the call.
  • Hindi opening: Namaste, Rupeezy se bol raha hoon. Kya main aapke 30 seconds le sakta hoon?
  • English opening: Hi, this is Rupeezy. Can I take 30 seconds to explain why I am calling?
  • Gujarati opening: Kem cho, hu Rupeezy mathi bolu chhu. Ek minute batavo?
  • Tamil opening: Vanakkam, naan Rupeezy-ilirundhu pesuren. Oru nimidam kuduthaalaam?
  • Marathi opening: Namaskar, mi Rupeezy kadun bolat ahe. 30 second milu shakta ka?
  • Hinglish opening: Namaste, Rupeezy se — quick 30 seconds?

  • Call me later / busy right now.
  • Trust / safety concerns.
  • Pricing / exact numbers — ask to verify via WhatsApp instead of guessing.
  • Send details first — send a short summary and confirm the next step.
  • Not interested.
